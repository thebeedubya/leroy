# Training Persona Definitions

Three audiences consume the same curriculum with different framing, depth, and outcomes.

---

## Persona 1: Partner

**Who they are:** Channel partners, cultivation consultants, hydro retail advisors, nutrient technical teams, influential growers. They sell AROYA or recommend it to growers. They are NOT Addium employees.

**What they need:** Enough technical knowledge to be credible with growers, plus the sales framework to convert conversations into pilot deployments.

**Framing:** "Here's how to explain this to a grower so they trust you know your stuff."

**Depth:** Conceptual understanding + demonstration ability. They need to explain VWC, EC, dryback, and crop steering without reading from a script. They do NOT need to configure the system or troubleshoot hardware.

**Tone:** Peer educator. Consultant-to-grower. Confident, practical, no jargon that the grower wouldn't use.

**Call to action:** Recommend a pilot. Get the grower to commit to 4-6 sensors in one room for one cycle.

**What's included:**
- Market context and competitive positioning
- Technology explanation (what it measures, why it matters)
- Crop steering concepts (vegetative vs generative, irrigation strategy)
- Opportunity identification signals
- Discovery question framework
- The AROYA pitch (60-second elevator version)
- Deal structure (pilot sizing, pricing, expansion path)
- Partner economics (commission structure, certification benefits)

**What's excluded:**
- Internal Addium metrics or targets
- Pricing negotiation authority or discount structures
- Customer onboarding procedures
- Platform administration or technical support workflows
- Competitive intelligence beyond positioning statements

---

## Persona 2: Customer

**Who they are:** Growers, cultivation directors, facility managers, IPM leads, irrigation technicians. They have purchased or are evaluating AROYA. They will use the platform daily.

**What they need:** Deep understanding of how to use AROYA to improve their cultivation outcomes. Technical proficiency with the platform, data interpretation skills, and actionable strategies they can implement immediately.

**Framing:** "Here's how AROYA gives you control over your cultivation outcomes."

**Depth:** Deep technical. They need to understand the biology behind crop steering, interpret every graph on the dashboard, set up irrigation strategies per growth phase, and use the data to make daily decisions. This is practitioner-level training.

**Tone:** Product guide. AROYA teaching you. Authoritative but not condescending. Respects their cultivation experience while introducing data-driven methods.

**Call to action:** Set up your first crop steering strategy in AROYA. Apply what you learned in your next irrigation cycle.

**What's included:**
- Industry context (why data-driven cultivation is the future)
- Full technology explanation (sensors, environment, gateway architecture)
- Platform deep dive (dashboard, graphs, alerts, historical comparison)
- Crop steering biology and practical application
- Phase-by-phase irrigation strategy with specific targets
- Data interpretation exercises with real AROYA graphs
- Troubleshooting common cultivation scenarios using AROYA data

**What's excluded:**
- Partner economics or commission structures
- Sales methodology or competitive positioning
- Internal Addium business metrics
- Deal strategy or pricing structures
- Partner certification pathway details

---

## Persona 3: Internal (Sales Team)

**Who they are:** Addium account executives, SDRs, sales engineers, customer success managers. They sell AROYA directly or support the sales process.

**What they need:** Enough technical knowledge to position AROYA credibly, handle objections, run discovery calls, and differentiate against competitors. They do NOT need to be cultivation experts, but they need to speak the language.

**Framing:** "Here's how to position this against competitors and close deals."

**Depth:** Enough to position, not enough to implement. They need to explain crop steering at a conceptual level, run a compelling demo, handle the top 10 objections, and structure deals. They defer deep technical questions to customer success or solutions engineering.

**Tone:** Sales enablement coach. Direct, tactical, focused on what moves deals forward. Brad's voice.

**Call to action:** Use this in your next discovery call. Apply the framework on your next facility visit.

**What's included:**
- Market context with competitive landscape (Growlink, Priva, Grodan, manual methods)
- Technology overview (enough to demo and explain, not troubleshoot)
- Platform demonstration skills (what to show, in what order, what to skip)
- Crop steering as a differentiator (the conversation that wins technical credibility)
- Opportunity qualification criteria
- Discovery framework with specific questions and desired answers
- The AROYA pitch (multiple versions: 30-second, 60-second, 5-minute)
- Objection handling (top 10 objections with responses)
- Deal strategy (pilot structure, pricing, expansion triggers, competitive displacement)
- Rep performance benchmarks and accountability metrics

**What's excluded:**
- Customer onboarding procedures (that's CS, not sales)
- Deep technical troubleshooting
- Partner commission structures or channel economics
- Platform configuration or administration

---

## Cross-Persona Adaptation Rules

1. **Never mix audiences.** Partner content does not reference internal metrics. Customer content does not reference partner economics. Internal content does not include customer onboarding steps.

2. **Same facts, different emphasis.** All three personas learn that VWC measures water content in the substrate. Partner learns "explain this so the grower trusts you." Customer learns "use this to decide when to irrigate." Internal learns "use this in demo to show real-time visibility competitors can't match."

3. **Assessment questions test persona-appropriate skills.** Partner: "How would you explain this to a grower?" Customer: "What would you do with this data?" Internal: "How would you position this against Competitor X?"

4. **Speaker notes are persona-specific coaching.** Partner notes coach on credibility. Customer notes coach on application. Internal notes coach on selling.

5. **Every module ends with a persona-appropriate action item.** Partner: something to do on their next facility visit. Customer: something to configure or try in AROYA. Internal: something to use on their next sales call.
